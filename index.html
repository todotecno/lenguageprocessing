03 Does the brain produce and comprehend language in different sectors?

No. Even though traditional models like the WLG got to this conclusion, new findings suggest otherwise. Hagoort and Indefrey (2014) identified two main processes:

Syntactic processing

Understanding grammatical structure and how words relate to each other.

Semantic processing

Understanding the meaning of words and sentences.

They highlight three important developments:

Language unification in the brain

Hover a zone: syntactic processing (grammar and sentence structure) sits in Broca’s area, BA 44; semantic processing (meaning of words and sentences) in BA 45 and BA 47.

Also observed in the left posterior temporal cortex: a similar dorsal (syntactic) vs. ventral (semantic) distinction.

04 Slips of the tongue

06 Specific vocabulary

Lexicon

The mental dictionary stored in our brain, including information about its meaning, pronunciation, and grammatical properties.

Morphemes

The smallest meaningful units of language. They can be complete or parts of words that add meaning or information.

Phonemes

The smallest distinctive sound units in a language. They are abstract mental representations of sounds that can distinguish one word from another.

Utterance

A piece of language produced by a speaker in a particular context. Its meaning can depend on the situation in which it is produced.
